Oil and gas is the largest industry behind all three men. Oil and gas donors have given Greg Abbott $82.8 million since 2001, Dan Patrick $18.8 million since 2008 and Ken Paxton's own campaigns $10.8 million since 2002. The next-largest industry gave each of them less: securities and investment gave Abbott $48.5 million, real estate gave Patrick $12.0 million, and securities and investment gave Paxton $5.6 million.

This election cycle, the pattern holds for Abbott and Patrick. Oil and gas supplied 16.8% of the $117.6 million Abbott has raised since January 2025, and 17.8% of Patrick's $26.5 million. Paxton's three committees took more from tech, almost all of it one $10 million check from Elon Musk to the super PAC backing his Senate run. Without that gift, oil and gas leads.

Every figure on this page comes from the filings. We counted every itemized contribution in the Texas Ethics Commission's records for each man's campaign committee and, for Paxton's Senate run, the Federal Election Commission's. Our rows match the filed totals on 77 of 77 of Abbott's state reports, 66 of 66 of Patrick's and 71 of 78 of Paxton's, to the cent, and on 20 of 20 of Paxton's federal filings. Each oil and gas donor below shows why it counts, and the data files carry the contribution ids to look up.

How this was counted

Method

What counts as oil and gas. Producers, oilfield service companies, royalty and mineral owners, refiners, pipelines, petrochemical makers, fuel wholesalers and haulers, their trade groups and political committees, and the families and investment vehicles that hold oil fortunes. Retail convenience stores are not counted. Utilities, power generators and diversified fortunes with an oil arm are kept apart as other energy.

How each donor was classified. Three passes, kept separate in the data: words in the filed employer and occupation, a list of known oil and gas companies, executives and families, and a review of the largest donors, each with a written reason. Reviews ran from the largest donor down.

Which money is counted. Every itemized contribution to each man's main campaign committee, including in-kind gifts, net of superseded reports. For Paxton, his state candidate account and, in 2026, his Senate campaign and joint fundraising committee; the tables also show a version that adds the super PAC backing him. Transfers between a man's own committees are not counted twice. Abbott's judicial campaigns of the 1990s and his officeholder account are not included.

Other industries. Assigned from the filed employer or, for the largest donors, by review, using OpenSecrets' categories. Committees that pass money along are counted as committees, not traced to their own donors.

Each file below is a plain spreadsheet. To check a total, keep the oil and gas rows for one committee in the contributions file and add up the amounts. To check one gift, search the filer id on the Texas Ethics Commission site, open the report and find the contribution id.

Using this data. Federal law bars using the names of individual contributors taken from FEC reports to solicit contributions or for any commercial purpose. Use these files for reporting, research and checking. They carry no street addresses or phone numbers. Federal committees may plant up to ten fictitious names in their reports to catch misuse, so a federal row could be a decoy.
